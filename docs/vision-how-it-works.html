Our Vision

Why we built the
AI Automation Report

AI is reshaping every industry — but most organisations are navigating its impact without a clear picture of what it means for their specific workforce. Generic market reports don't answer the questions HR and business leaders actually face: Which roles are changing? Where is the risk? Where is the opportunity?

Organisations are flying blind

The vast majority of AI transformation decisions are being made on intuition, analyst averages, or industry benchmarks that don't reflect a company's actual job architecture. An "average" automation score for "Financial Services" tells you very little about the specific mix of roles your organisation employs today.

Business leaders deserve role-level intelligence — grounded in their own job descriptions, not someone else's workforce.

The gap between potential and readiness

We built this report to close that gap — turning publicly available job posting data into a structured, company-specific AI readiness picture that organisations can act on immediately.

Methodology

How the
AI Automation Report
is built

Every report is generated through a four-stage pipeline that turns publicly available job posting data into structured, role-level AI automation intelligence — specific to your organisation.

1

Data sourcing — public job postings

We collect publicly available job postings associated with your organisation's careers page. These postings are the authoritative, up-to-date description of what your organisation actually needs from its workforce — more current than internal job catalogues and more specific than O*NET or industry surveys.

We do not use confidential HR data, internal salary information, or any non-public employee records. The analysis is built entirely from information your organisation has already made public.

Public careers pages Job descriptions No proprietary data required
2

Task & skill extraction

Each job description is processed by iMocha's AI analysis pipeline, which decomposes the role into its constituent tasks, required skills, and implied responsibilities. Tasks are normalised across roles so that, for example, "data reconciliation" described differently across five finance roles is mapped to a consistent underlying activity.

Skills are mapped to iMocha's proprietary skills taxonomy — one of the largest in the industry — which classifies competencies across technical, cognitive, interpersonal, and domain-specific dimensions.

Task decomposition Skill mapping Cross-role normalisation
3

Automation scoring

Each extracted task is evaluated against a multi-factor automation model that considers:

  • Cognitive complexity — degree of judgment, creativity, and novel problem-solving required
  • Predictability — whether the task follows well-defined rules or requires contextual adaptation
  • Data intensity — how much of the task involves processing structured information
  • Human interaction requirement — degree to which emotional intelligence, trust, or physical presence is intrinsic to the task
  • Current AI capability — assessed against the state of commercially available AI tools at the time of analysis

Tasks are classified into three categories, and an overall automation score (0–100) is computed per role as a weighted aggregate:

Low automation potential

Tasks that rely heavily on human judgment, creativity, relational skills, or physical dexterity. AI can assist but not replace the core activity.

Medium — AI-augmentable

Tasks where AI tools can meaningfully accelerate output, reduce error rates, or shift effort — but human oversight and interpretation remain essential.

High automation potential

Structured, rule-based tasks with well-defined inputs and outputs that current AI systems can perform with minimal human intervention.

4

Company-level aggregation

Individual role scores are aggregated across your organisation's full job architecture to produce the company-level metrics shown in your report:

Department-level breakdowns are computed by grouping roles according to the department or function indicated in each job posting. Peer comparisons use an illustrative industry baseline to provide directional context — they are not derived from confidential competitor data.

All figures are estimates based on job description analysis at a point in time. They should be treated as indicative insights to inform strategic discussion, not as precise operational measurements.

What we don't do

Being clear about the boundaries of this analysis is as important as what it delivers.

✕

We do not access internal HR systems or proprietary employee data. The analysis is based solely on publicly available job postings.

✕

We do not make predictions about specific employees. Scores apply to roles and tasks — never to individuals.

✕

We do not guarantee specific business outcomes. Estimated hours saved and efficiency gains are modelled projections, not guaranteed results.

✕

We do not claim that AI will replace specific roles. Automation scores reflect task-level potential — how AI can augment or assist, not a forecast of headcount reduction.
